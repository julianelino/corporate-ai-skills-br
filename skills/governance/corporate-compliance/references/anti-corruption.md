# Anti-Corruption (Lei Anticorrupção — Lei 12.846/2013)

## Topics Covered
- anti-corruption

## Purpose
Determine how Lei 12.846/2013's objective-liability framework and Decreto 11.129/2022's integrity-program criteria bear on a specific compliance question — before assessing a conduct pattern, control, or program element against the anti-corruption framework specifically.

## When to Load
Load whenever a request involves a potential act of corruption against public administration (bribery, improper advantage to a public official, fraud in public procurement/contracts), or an assessment of the company's own programa de integridade (compliance program) as a mitigating factor — before characterizing conduct or program adequacy against this specific legal framework.

## Scope
Covers Lei nº 12.846/2013 ("Lei Anticorrupção" / "Lei da Empresa Limpa") and Decreto nº 11.129/2022 (its current implementing regulation, replacing the earlier Decreto 8.420/2015). Does not cover the general conflict-of-interest or gift-policy frameworks this skill covers separately (see `conflict-of-interest.md`, `gifts.md`, though both intersect heavily with anti-corruption where a public official is involved) or the individual criminal liability of specific persons (Lei 12.846/2013 targets the legal entity's administrative/civil liability; individual criminal exposure is a separate legal question).

## Core Concepts
- **Responsabilidade objetiva (objective/strict liability)**: Lei 12.846/2013's defining feature — a legal entity can be held administratively and civilly liable for a corrupt act committed in its interest or for its benefit, without needing to prove the company's intent or fault (a materially lower bar than the individual-liability standard in general criminal law) — this means a compliance assessment cannot rely on "the company didn't intend this" as a defense; the focus shifts to whether the act occurred in the company's interest/benefit and what the company's integrity program did to prevent/detect it.
- **Scope — acts against public administration**: the law targets acts of corruption specifically against domestic or foreign public administration (bribery of public officials, fraud in public bidding/contracts, obstruction of investigation/oversight, among others) — a purely private-sector bribery/kickback scenario (no public-official or public-administration dimension) falls outside this specific law's scope, though it may still implicate other legal frameworks and this skill's general conduct/conflict-of-interest references.
- **Programa de integridade as a mitigating (not exculpatory) factor**: Decreto 11.129/2022 establishes the criteria for evaluating an integrity program's effectiveness when determining sanctions — a genuinely effective program can reduce sanctions, but does not eliminate the underlying objective liability if a violation occurred; the assessment question is whether the program's existence and effectiveness should mitigate the consequence, not whether it prevents liability from attaching in the first place.
- **Key integrity-program elements (per Decreto 11.129/2022, as identified in this research)**: "tone from the top" (documented, practical evidence that senior leadership genuinely supports and applies integrity rules — not merely a signed policy); and effective whistleblowing channels open to employees and third parties, guaranteeing anonymity and protection against retaliation (directly connecting to `retaliation.md` in this skill). - **Decreto 11.129/2022 — the current regulation**: this decree revoked and replaced the earlier Decreto 8.420/2015, introducing changes to the preliminary-investigation phase, applicable fines, leniency-agreement (acordo de leniência) mechanics, and integrity-program evaluation criteria — an analysis referencing "Decreto 8.420" as the current regulation reflects outdated information and should be updated to Decreto 11.129/2022.
- **Acordo de leniência (leniency agreement)**: a mechanism allowing a company to cooperate with authorities (disclosing the violation, providing evidence, ceasing involvement) in exchange for sanction reduction — a specific legal process requiring dedicated legal counsel, not something this skill's general methodology resolves; flag its potential relevance and escalate rather than attempting to structure one.

## Decision Points
1. Does the conduct in question involve public administration (a public official, government contract/procurement, or public-sector interaction), bringing it within Lei 12.846/2013's specific scope — or is it a private-sector-only matter falling under this skill's general conduct/conflict-of-interest frameworks instead?
2. If within scope, does the objective-liability standard apply regardless of proof of the company's intent — meaning the assessment should focus on the act's occurrence and the company's-interest/benefit nexus, not on establishing corporate "guilt" in the traditional sense?
3. Is the company's current integrity program's adequacy relevant to this assessment (e.g., because a violation has been identified and sanction-mitigation is now a live question), requiring evaluation against Decreto 11.129/2022's current criteria?
4. Does the specific integrity-program element in question (tone from the top, whistleblowing channel) meet the "genuinely effective, not merely nominal" standard this framework requires?
5. Does the situation's severity/stage suggest a leniency-agreement question requiring dedicated legal escalation, beyond this skill's own compliance-assessment authority?

## Required Facts
- Whether a public official or public administration is involved in the conduct being assessed.
- Whether the conduct occurred in the company's interest or for its benefit (the objective-liability nexus).
- The current state of the company's integrity program relative to Decreto 11.129/2022's criteria, where program-adequacy is the question.
- Whether the matter's stage/severity raises a leniency-agreement question.

## Required Evidence
- Documentation of the specific conduct and its public-administration/public-official connection.
- Integrity-program documentation (policies, training records, whistleblowing-channel usage/protection records) where program-adequacy is being assessed.
- Any prior similar findings or sanctions, for pattern/severity context.

## Exceptions
- A gift or courtesy to a public official that falls within a specific, narrow, officially-recognized exception (e.g., an institutional/ceremonial gift of nominal value, where such an exception exists in the specific jurisdiction's own rules) is not automatically a violation — but this exception should be verified against the specific applicable rule, never assumed. ## Risk Considerations
- Assuming a lack of proven intent is a defense misunderstands the objective-liability standard — this is a materially different risk posture than general criminal-liability analysis, and treating it the same way understates the company's exposure.
- Treating a nominally-existing but non-functional integrity-program element (a whistleblowing channel that exists but isn't genuinely protected/used, a code of conduct signed but not reinforced by leadership behavior) as satisfying Decreto 11.129/2022's criteria overstates the program's actual mitigating value.
- Referencing the superseded Decreto 8.420/2015 rather than the current Decreto 11.129/2022 risks applying an outdated evaluation framework to a program-adequacy assessment.
