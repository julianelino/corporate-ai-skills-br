# Interviews

## Topics Covered
- interviews

## Purpose
Establish the methodology for planning and conducting investigative interviews — the PEACE model's non-accusatory, information-gathering approach, and the accused's right to know the allegation and be heard — before interview topics are prepared or an interview is conducted.

## When to Load
Load whenever a request involves planning or evaluating an investigative interview — witness, reporter, or accused — before preparing interview topics or drawing conclusions from an interview record.

## Scope
Covers interview methodology: structure, technique, and the accused's procedural rights. Does not cover the general evidence-weighing framework interview-derived evidence feeds into (see `evidence.md`) or the preservation of interview records themselves (see `preservation.md`).

## Core Concepts
- **PEACE model — non-accusatory, information-gathering structure**: a recognized best-practice investigative-interviewing framework (developed originally in England/Wales, per this research, now widely adopted in professional investigative practice) structured in five phases — Planning & Preparation, Engage & Explain, Account, Closure, Evaluate — designed to obtain a full, accurate account of events rather than to extract a confession. This skill's own SKILL.md prohibition on "coaching evidence" aligns directly with this model's core principle: the interview's purpose is gathering an accurate account, not steering the interviewee toward a predetermined conclusion.
- **Planning & Preparation**: before the interview, define its specific purpose within the investigation's scope (per `allegation.md`), what is already known (per the current evidence ledger), and the topics to cover — an unprepared interview risks missing material topics or, conversely, wandering beyond the defined scope.
- **Engage & Explain**: establish rapport and clearly explain the interview's purpose and ground rules (confidentiality limits, the interviewee's role — witness, reporter, or accused — and what that role does and doesn't mean about any conclusion) before substantive questioning begins.
- **Account, Closure, Evaluate**: the substantive phase (obtaining the interviewee's account, using open questions before specific ones, avoiding leading questions that suggest the desired answer), followed by a structured close (summarizing back to confirm accuracy, addressing any final points) and post-interview evaluation (assessing what was learned against the existing evidence ledger, identifying follow-up needs).
- **The accused's specific procedural rights**: per this research's recognized best-practice framing, no action materially affecting an individual as a result of investigation findings should be implemented without that individual having had the opportunity to (1) know the specific allegation against them, (2) be heard — give their own account and have it genuinely considered, and (3) be judged on the evidence, not on assumption or pressure. This right-to-respond principle should be treated as a standard procedural requirement before a consequential finding is finalized, not an optional courtesy.
- **Leading and coaching are prohibited, not just discouraged**: a question that suggests its own answer, or any attempt to guide a witness toward a specific account, compromises both the interview's reliability and, per this skill's own explicit prohibition, its legitimacy — this applies to interviewer conduct toward witnesses and toward the accused alike.

## Decision Points
1. Has the interview's specific purpose, scope, and topic list been prepared in advance, grounded in the current evidence ledger and the defined allegation scope?
2. Has the interviewee's role (witness, reporter, accused) and the interview's ground rules (confidentiality limits, no predetermined conclusion) been clearly explained before substantive questioning?
3. Are questions open and non-leading, avoiding any suggestion of the desired or expected answer?
4. For an accused individual specifically: has the specific allegation been disclosed to them, and have they had a genuine opportunity to respond before any finding is finalized?
5. Has the interview record been evaluated against the existing evidence ledger post-interview, identifying corroboration, contradiction, or follow-up needs (per `evidence.md`)?

## Required Facts
- The interview's defined purpose, scope, and prepared topic list.
- The interviewee's role and what ground rules were explained to them.
- Whether, for an accused individual, the specific allegation was disclosed and a genuine response opportunity given.
- Post-interview evaluation of how the account fits the existing evidence ledger.

## Required Evidence
- The interview record (notes, recording where permitted and consented to, or a structured summary).
- Documentation that ground rules and, for an accused individual, the specific allegation were communicated.
- The pre-interview topic plan, for confirming the interview stayed within defined scope.

## Exceptions
- An interview may need to depart from a strict phase sequence where the interviewee volunteers material information early — flexibility in structure is appropriate, but the underlying principles (non-leading questions, full opportunity to give an account, no coaching) should still govern regardless of sequence.
- A reporter/complainant interview may not require the same "opportunity to respond to an allegation" framing an accused-individual interview requires (since they are not the accused) — but should still follow the same non-leading, accurate-account-gathering discipline.

## Risk Considerations
- Leading or coaching a witness (even unintentionally, through phrasing or tone) compromises the interview's evidentiary reliability and, per this skill's explicit prohibition, its legitimacy.
- Failing to disclose the specific allegation to an accused individual, or failing to genuinely consider their account, risks both a fairness failure and a findings determination vulnerable to later challenge.
- Treating an embedded assertion within an interview (a witness stating "everyone knows the accused is guilty") as itself evidence of guilt, rather than as one data point to weigh per `evidence.md`'s discipline, undermines the entire ledger framework.

## Human Escalation Conditions
Escalate for human legal/investigative leadership review whenever: the interviewee is senior leadership or otherwise presents a power-dynamic complication; the accused disputes having received adequate notice/opportunity to respond; an interview reveals information suggesting the investigation's scope should expand; or legal privilege/representation questions arise for the interviewee.
