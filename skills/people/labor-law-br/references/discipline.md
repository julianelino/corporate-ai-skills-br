# Discipline (Poder Disciplinar e Justa Causa)

## Topics Covered
- discipline

## Purpose
Determine whether a specific employee conduct supports a disciplinary measure, which measure (advertência, suspensão, or dispensa por justa causa) is proportionate, and whether procedural requirements (proportionality, imediatidade, non bis in idem, evidence) are satisfied before the measure is applied.

## When to Load
Load whenever a request involves punishing, warning, suspending, or dismissing an employee for alleged misconduct, or evaluating whether a past disciplinary action was valid — before recommending or drafting any disciplinary document.

## Scope
Covers CLT Art. 482 (justa causa grounds) and the judicially-developed doctrine governing the employer's poder disciplinar (proportionality/gradação, imediatidade, non bis in idem). Does not cover the verbas consequences of a termination (see `termination.md`) or the evidentiary handling of a disciplinary dispute once litigated (see `labor-litigation.md`) or investigated (see `corporate-investigation` skill for a formal internal investigation).

## Core Concepts
- **Poder disciplinar**: the employer's authority, inherent in the subordination that defines an employment relationship, to apply proportionate corrective measures for employee misconduct — not an unlimited or arbitrary power.
- **Gradação/proporcionalidade**: Brazilian labor doctrine and jurisprudence generally expect discipline to escalate — advertência (verbal or written warning) before suspensão, before dispensa por justa causa — matched to the severity and recurrence of the conduct. Some grounds (e.g., ato de improbidade) are considered severe enough to justify immediate justa causa without a prior warning; whether a given fact pattern requires prior gradation is doctrinally and evidentially fact-specific, not a fixed rule this reference can resolve — treat as requiring case-by-case, evidence-based judgment, and escalate when material.
- **Art. 482 grounds (rol taxativo)**: labor courts treat Art. 482's list as exhaustive — conduct not falling within one of its enumerated categories cannot support justa causa, however undesirable. Categories include (illustrative, not exhaustive of this reference's own coverage): ato de improbidade; incontinência de conduta ou mau procedimento; negociação habitual sem permissão e concorrencial ou prejudicial ao serviço; condenação criminal transitada em julgado sem suspensão da pena; desídia; embriaguez habitual ou em serviço; violação de segredo da empresa; ato de indisciplina ou insubordinação; abandono de emprego; ato lesivo da honra ou ofensas físicas contra empregador/superiores; prática constante de jogos de azar; and additional grounds addressing national-security conduct and other categories — confirm the exact current enumeration and lettering against a current T1 source before citing a specific alínea in a consequential document, since this reference does not reproduce the full statutory list verbatim (see Known Limitations).
- **Princípio da imediatidade**: a disciplinary measure — especially justa causa — must be applied within a reasonable time after the employer becomes aware of the misconduct. Jurisprudence commonly treats delay as evidence of **perdão tácito** (tacit forgiveness), which discharacterizes the justa causa. What counts as "reasonable" is fact- and forum-specific (larger organizations with internal investigation/compliance procedures are generally given more latitude before the clock is treated as having run) — do not assume a fixed day count; check current TST/TRT guidance for the specific fact pattern.
- **Non bis in idem**: an employer cannot punish the same conduct twice (e.g., a written warning followed by a suspension for the identical, already-sanctioned incident) — a second, escalated measure requires either a new incident or a continuation/aggravation of the same misconduct after the first measure, not a re-punishment of the same closed episode.

## Decision Points
1. Does the alleged conduct map to a specific Art. 482 ground (or, for a lesser measure, simply constitute misconduct warranting advertência/suspensão)? Do not treat undesirable-but-uncategorized conduct as justa causa-eligible.
2. Has the employer already sanctioned this specific incident? If yes, a further sanction for the same episode risks non bis in idem exposure.
3. How much time elapsed between the employer's actual knowledge of the misconduct and the proposed measure? Does the elapsed time plausibly support a perdão tácito challenge?
4. Is the proposed measure proportionate to the severity and recurrence of the conduct, or does it skip gradation without a doctrinal basis for doing so (e.g., a severe grounds like ato de improbidade)?
5. What evidence supports the alleged conduct, and has the employee had a documented opportunity to respond before a consequential measure is finalized? `CORPORATE_CONTEXT_REQUIRED` for the applicable internal disciplinary policy/matrix, if one exists.

## Required Facts
- The specific conduct alleged, with date(s).
- Date the employer (the relevant decision-maker, not just any employee) became aware of the conduct.
- Any prior disciplinary history for the same or related conduct.
- The measure being considered (advertência, suspensão, or dispensa por justa causa) and its proportionality relative to the conduct and history.
- Applicable internal disciplinary policy or matrix, if the company has one. `CORPORATE_CONTEXT_REQUIRED`.

## Required Evidence
- Documentation of the misconduct (incident reports, witness statements, system logs, or equivalent) sufficient to substantiate the specific Art. 482 ground or lesser-measure basis being relied on.
- Timeline evidence establishing when the employer became aware, to assess imediatidade.
- Evidence the employee had an opportunity to respond, where the internal process requires it. `CORPORATE_CONTEXT_REQUIRED` for the specific process.
- Prior disciplinary record, to assess proportionality and non bis in idem.

## Exceptions
- Severe grounds (e.g., ato de improbidade, abandono de emprego with clear evidentiary support) are generally treated as not requiring prior gradation — but this is a fact-specific determination, not a blanket rule to apply without checking the specific ground and evidentiary strength.
- A long internal investigation conducted through a documented, good-faith compliance process is generally given more latitude on imediatidade than an ad hoc delay — but "documented, good-faith process" is itself a fact question requiring evidence, not an assumption.

## Risk Considerations
- Applying justa causa without first checking that the conduct maps to an actual Art. 482 ground is a common, high-risk error — courts routinely reverse justa causa for exactly this reason, converting it (with back-pay consequences) into a dispensa sem justa causa.
- Delaying a disciplinary measure without documenting the reason for the delay creates real perdão tácito exposure — this is a frequently litigated fact pattern.
- Applying an escalated measure for an already-sanctioned incident (double jeopardy) is both a doctrinal violation and commonly successful in litigation against the employer.

## Human Escalation Conditions
Escalate for human legal review whenever: dispensa por justa causa is being considered (highest-consequence measure); the underlying conduct is disputed or evidentially thin; imediatidade is arguably compromised by delay; the conduct potentially also implicates harassment, discrimination, or a formal investigation (route to `employee-relations` and/or `corporate-investigation`); or the employee holds any stability/guarantee (see `termination.md`).
