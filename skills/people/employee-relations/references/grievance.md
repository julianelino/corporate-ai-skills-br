# Grievance (Canal de Queixas e Conciliação)

## Topics Covered
- grievance

## Purpose
Structure how an employee's internal complaint or grievance (about treatment, a decision, a policy application, or a conflict) is received, triaged, and resolved through internal channels — and clarify how internal grievance handling relates to, but does not substitute for, the employee's independent right to seek judicial or extrajudicial labor remedies.

## When to Load
Load whenever a request involves an employee-raised complaint that is not itself framed as a harassment, discrimination, or formal-investigation matter (those have dedicated references) — including questions about internal grievance channel design, triage, or an employee's parallel use of an external mechanism like a Comissão de Conciliação Prévia (CCP).

## Scope
Covers internal grievance-channel process design and triage principles, and CLT Arts. 625-A–625-H (Comissão de Conciliação Prévia) as the relevant extrajudicial labor-dispute mechanism. Does not cover harassment-specific reporting channels (see `harassment.md`, which covers the Lei 14.457/2022 mandatory channel), formal investigations (see `investigation.md`), or litigation once a claim is filed (see `labor-law-br`'s `labor-litigation.md`).

## Core Concepts
- **Internal grievance channel**: a mechanism (HR intake, ombudsman/canal de ética, or direct manager escalation) through which an employee raises a concern that does not necessarily allege legal wrongdoing but reflects a conflict, a disputed decision, or a policy-application concern. Triage should determine whether the matter is a routine grievance, or whether it in fact implicates harassment, discrimination, or conduct warranting formal investigation — in which case it should be routed to the corresponding reference/skill rather than handled as an ordinary grievance.
- **Comissão de Conciliação Prévia — CCP (CLT Arts. 625-A–625-H)**: a voluntary, parity-composed (equal employer/employee representation) extrajudicial commission that companies or unions may establish to attempt conciliation of individual labor disputes before litigation. Where a CCP exists and is used, it has 10 days from the request to hold a conciliation session; a successful conciliation term has general liberatory effect as an extrajudicial enforceable instrument (except for expressly excepted items); a failed attempt produces a "declaração de tentativa conciliatória frustrada" the employee can attach to a subsequent labor claim.
- **CCP is not mandatory**: the STF has held that submitting a dispute to a CCP before filing a labor claim is not a mandatory precondition to accessing the Justiça do Trabalho — an employee's right to sue is not conditioned on first exhausting a CCP process, even where the company has one. Do not represent CCP use as a required step before litigation.
- **Internal resolution does not waive external rights**: resolving a grievance internally (or an employee declining to use an internal channel) does not by itself extinguish or waive the employee's independent right to pursue a labor claim, a CCP conciliation, or an external regulatory complaint — do not frame internal resolution as legally foreclosing those paths unless a specific, valid conciliation term with liberatory effect was actually executed.

## Decision Points
1. Does the grievance, on triage, actually implicate harassment, discrimination, or conduct warranting formal investigation? If so, route to the corresponding reference rather than treating it as a routine grievance.
2. Is there an applicable CCP (company- or union-level) relevant to this matter, and if so, is the employee independently choosing to use it — recognizing this is optional, not a gate on the employee's other rights?
3. What is the appropriate internal owner and resolution timeline for this specific grievance, given its nature and severity?
4. Does resolving the grievance require a decision that itself needs the disciplinary (`discipline.md`) or investigation (`investigation.md`) process, or can it be resolved through direct conflict-resolution/mediation?
5. Has the grievant been given clear information about their independent right to escalate externally (CCP, labor claim, regulatory complaint) regardless of the internal process's outcome?

- The internal channel's applicable resolution timeline and owner. `CORPORATE_CONTEXT_REQUIRED`.
- Any prior related grievances from the same employee or about the same underlying matter, for consistency and pattern context.

## Required Evidence
- The grievance intake record itself (what was reported, when, and through which channel).
- Any resolution or response provided, and whether the grievant was informed of external escalation options.
- CCP session records and any conciliation term, if that path was used.

## Exceptions
- A grievance that on its face or after triage reveals harassment, discrimination, or conduct requiring formal investigation should not be resolved through ordinary grievance channels alone — reroute per Decision Point 1.
- An employee's choice not to use an internal grievance channel at all, and to go directly to an external mechanism (CCP, labor claim, regulatory complaint), is a valid exercise of their independent rights and should not be treated as a procedural failure on the employee's part.

## Risk Considerations
- Representing CCP submission as mandatory, or as a precondition to a labor claim, misstates current STF doctrine and could improperly discourage or delay an employee's access to justice.
- Treating an internal grievance resolution as extinguishing the employee's external rights, absent an actual valid conciliation term with liberatory effect, overstates the internal process's legal effect.
- Failing to triage a grievance for harassment/discrimination/investigation-level content, and instead processing it only as routine conflict, risks mishandling a matter that required the more rigorous evidentiary and procedural safeguards of `investigation.md`.

## Human Escalation Conditions
Escalate for human review whenever: triage suggests the grievance may implicate harassment, discrimination, or conduct warranting formal investigation; a CCP conciliation term is being drafted or finalized (its liberatory effect has real legal consequences); or the grievance involves a pattern suggesting a systemic issue rather than an isolated conflict.
