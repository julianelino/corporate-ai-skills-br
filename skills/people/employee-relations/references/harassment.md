# Harassment (Assédio Moral e Sexual)

## Topics Covered
- harassment

## Purpose
Distinguish assédio moral (moral/psychological harassment) and assédio sexual (sexual harassment) from ordinary workplace friction or legitimate managerial conduct, identify the mandatory prevention/reporting obligations that apply, and structure an appropriate intake and response — before any conclusion about whether alleged conduct constitutes harassment.

## When to Load
Load whenever a request involves an allegation, pattern, or risk of moral or sexual harassment — including a request to design or evaluate a harassment-prevention channel or policy — before characterizing conduct as harassment or structuring a response.

## Scope
Covers Lei nº 10.224/2001 (assédio sexual as a criminal offense), Lei nº 14.457/2022 (Programa Emprega + Mulheres — mandatory prevention measures and reporting channel for companies with a CIPA), and Portaria MTE nº 1.419/2024 (NR-1 Capítulo 1.5 — psychosocial risks, including harassment, in the PGR). Does not cover discrimination claims not framed as harassment (see `discrimination.md`), the disciplinary process for a substantiated case (see `discipline.md`), or formal investigation mechanics (see `investigation.md`).

## Core Concepts
- **Assédio moral — characterizing elements**: current doctrine and TST jurisprudence generally require (1) abusive conduct, (2) repetition/prolongation over time, and (3) an attack on the victim's psychological dignity — isolated, non-repeated friction does not typically meet the threshold. Recent case law has, in cases of exceptional severity, recognized a single sufficiently grave act as capable of constituting assédio moral by itself ("ato único qualificado") — this is an evolving, fact-intensive area; do not assume either the strict repeated-conduct requirement or the single-act exception applies without case-specific evaluation.
- **Assédio sexual (Lei 10.224/2001)**: criminalizes constrangimento with the purpose of obtaining a sexual advantage or favor, using the position's hierarchical superiority or the influence inherent to the function — a criminal-law concept that also grounds civil/labor consequences; a workplace policy response does not require waiting for a criminal conviction to act on a well-evidenced internal allegation.
- **Dano in re ipsa**: for genuinely severe harassment, TST jurisprudence has admitted that harm may be presumed from the gravity of the facts themselves, without requiring separate proof of the victim's subjective suffering — relevant to risk assessment, not a reason to skip evidentiary rigor at the intake stage.
- **Mandatory prevention infrastructure (Lei 14.457/2022)**: companies with a mandatory CIPA must implement measures to prevent sexual harassment and other forms of violence, including a reporting channel that is safe, confidential, and — where requested — anonymous, with allegations processed within a reasonable period (commonly cited as up to 30 days in secondary guidance; verify the current standard before committing to a specific SLA). `CORPORATE_CONTEXT_REQUIRED` for whether the specific company has a mandatory CIPA and what channel currently exists.
- **NR-1 / psychosocial risk integration (Portaria MTE 1.419/2024)**: harassment (moral and sexual) is treated as an occupational psychosocial risk factor that must be identified, assessed, controlled, and documented in the PGR (Programa de Gerenciamento de Riscos) — compliance with the Lei 14.457 reporting channel does not by itself satisfy the separate NR-1 risk-management documentation obligation, and vice versa.

## Decision Points
1. Does the alleged conduct show the pattern (abusive, repeated/prolonged, dignity-attacking) generally required for assédio moral, or could it instead qualify as an "ato único qualificado" given its severity? Treat as a fact-intensive determination, not a checklist.
2. Does the conduct involve a sexual element combined with a hierarchical-power dynamic, implicating Lei 10.224/2001's assédio sexual framework specifically (which carries additional criminal-referral considerations beyond the internal HR process)?
3. Was the allegation received through the mandatory reporting channel (if applicable), and is it being processed within the expected timeframe?
4. Does this allegation also trigger the NR-1 psychosocial-risk documentation obligation, independent of how the individual case is resolved?
5. Is there any indication of retaliation risk against the reporting employee, requiring the non-retaliation safeguards described in `discipline.md`?

## Required Facts
- The specific conduct alleged, its frequency/duration, and the relative positions (hierarchy) of the parties involved.
- Whether the conduct has a sexual-advantage/favor element specifically implicating Lei 10.224/2001.
- Whether the company has a mandatory CIPA and an established reporting channel, and whether this allegation came through it. `CORPORATE_CONTEXT_REQUIRED`.
- Any prior similar allegations involving the same parties, relevant to pattern assessment and consistency.

## Required Evidence
- Contemporaneous documentation of the alleged conduct (messages, witness accounts, incident reports).
- Reporting-channel records, if the allegation arrived through the mandatory channel.
- Any relevant PGR/psychosocial-risk documentation already in place. `CORPORATE_CONTEXT_REQUIRED`.

## Exceptions
- Legitimate, proportionate managerial feedback or performance correction — even if unwelcome to the recipient — is not automatically harassment; the abusive/dignity-attacking character is what distinguishes harassment from ordinary management, and this distinction requires careful, case-specific evaluation rather than a default in either direction.
- A single isolated incident that does not rise to the "ato único qualificado" severity threshold may still warrant a disciplinary or corrective response under `discipline.md` even if it does not itself constitute legally-actionable assédio moral — do not treat "not harassment" as "no action warranted."

## Risk Considerations
- Under-characterizing severe, repeated conduct as mere "workplace friction" exposes both the victim to continued harm and the company to liability, including potential dano in re ipsa exposure.
- Over-characterizing isolated managerial correction as harassment, without evidentiary basis, undermines legitimate management and can itself produce unfair-treatment claims from the accused.
- Failing to maintain the Lei 14.457 channel's confidentiality/anonymity commitments, or missing the reasonable-processing-time expectation, creates independent regulatory exposure distinct from the merits of any individual allegation.
- Treating Lei 14.457 channel compliance as satisfying the separate NR-1/PGR psychosocial-risk documentation obligation is a common, incorrect assumption.

## Human Escalation Conditions
Escalate for human review whenever: the allegation involves assédio sexual specifically (potential criminal-referral considerations); the conduct is severe enough to be argued as an "ato único qualificado"; retaliation risk against the reporter is plausible; the matter requires formal investigation (route to `investigation.md`); or the company's NR-1/PGR documentation obligations are unclear for this fact pattern.

## Freshness Requirements
Critical. The NR-1 psychosocial-risk obligation is recent (2024 portaria) and its enforcement/phase-in details are actively evolving; the Lei 14.457 channel's specific processing-time expectations should be re-verified against current MTE guidance before committing to a specific SLA in any consequential recommendation.

## Effective-Date Considerations
- **CURRENT (since 2022, Lei 14.457/2022):** mandatory harassment-prevention measures and reporting channel for companies with a mandatory CIPA.
- **CURRENT (since Portaria MTE 1.419/2024, in force 2024 with phase-in considerations):** psychosocial risks, including harassment, must be identified/assessed/documented in the PGR under the amended NR-1 Capítulo 1.5 — verify the specific current phase-in/enforcement timeline against `mte` before asserting a company is or is not yet subject to a specific documentation deadline.
- Lei 10.224/2001's criminalization of assédio sexual predates both of the above and remains CURRENT, unaffected by the more recent prevention-infrastructure laws.
