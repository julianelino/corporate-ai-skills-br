# Discipline (Processo Disciplinar Interno)

## Topics Covered
- discipline

## Purpose
Structure a fair, consistent, evidence-based internal disciplinary process — distinct from, and prerequisite to, the legal validity analysis of a specific measure under CLT Art. 482 (see `labor-law-br`'s `discipline.md`) — so that a recommended measure is proportionate, consistently applied, and procedurally defensible before it reaches a legal-validity or termination decision.

## When to Load
Load whenever a request involves recommending, structuring, or reviewing an internal disciplinary process (warning, suspension, or a recommendation toward dismissal) — before determining whether the specific facts pattern legally supports a given CLT sanction (that determination belongs to `labor-law-br`).

## Scope
Covers the HR-process dimension of workplace discipline: intake, evidence separation, proportionality, consistency across similar cases, the employee's right to respond, and the least-grave-effective-measure principle. Does not cover the CLT Art. 482 legal grounds themselves, imediatidade, or non bis in idem doctrine — those are covered in `labor-law-br`'s `discipline.md` and must be checked before any measure reaching dismissal-level severity is finalized. Does not cover harassment- or discrimination-specific allegations, which have their own reference files in this skill.

## Core Concepts
- **Separation of allegation, evidence, contradiction, and fact**: an HR-led disciplinary intake keeps these categories distinct throughout the process — an allegation is never treated as a confirmed fact merely because it was raised, and a confirmed fact is not diluted back into an allegation once evidenced. This mirrors the evidentiary discipline `corporate-investigation` uses for formal investigations, applied at HR-process scale for routine discipline.
- **Proportionality**: the recommended measure should match the severity, intent, impact, and recurrence of the conduct — a first minor infraction should not default to the most severe available measure, and a severe or repeated infraction should not be under-punished merely for administrative convenience.
- **Consistency**: similarly-situated conduct should receive similarly-situated treatment across employees and time; an HR process that cannot show consistent application invites both morale damage and legal exposure (disparate treatment is itself evidentiary ammunition in a later dispute).
- **Right to respond**: before a consequential measure is finalized, the employee should generally have a documented opportunity to give their account of the facts — this protects both fairness and the eventual evidentiary record if the measure is later contested.
- **Least-grave effective measure**: among the available measures that would adequately address the conduct, the process should recommend the least severe one that is actually effective — not the most severe one available "to be safe."
- **Non-retaliation**: an employee who reports misconduct, participates in an investigation, or exercises a legal right (e.g., disputing a prior measure) must not be subjected to a disciplinary measure motivated by that protected activity — any disciplinary action following closely after such activity warrants extra scrutiny for a retaliatory motive before proceeding.

## Decision Points
1. Is the underlying conduct clearly established as fact, or does it remain at the allegation/contradiction stage? Do not recommend a measure calibrated to a confirmed fact when the record only supports an allegation.
2. How has the organization treated comparable conduct in the past? Does the proposed measure remain consistent, or does it deviate — and if it deviates, is there a documented, legitimate reason?
3. Has the employee had a genuine, documented opportunity to respond to the specific allegation before the measure is finalized?
4. Among the available measures, which is the least severe one that would still adequately address the conduct and its risk?
5. Does the disciplinary action follow closely after the employee's protected activity (report, complaint, investigation participation, prior dispute)? If so, is there a legitimate, well-evidenced, non-retaliatory basis clearly documented?
6. Does the measure being considered rise to a level (suspension, dismissal-track) that requires legal-validity review under `labor-law-br`'s `discipline.md` before finalization?

## Required Facts
- The specific conduct alleged, with dates, and its evidentiary status (allegation, contradicted, or confirmed).
- Comparable prior cases and how they were resolved, for consistency review.
- Whether the employee has been given an opportunity to respond, and what that response was.
- Any recent protected activity by the employee (report, complaint, investigation involvement, prior legal dispute) that could raise a retaliation concern.
- Applicable internal disciplinary policy or matrix. `CORPORATE_CONTEXT_REQUIRED`.

## Required Evidence
- Documentation supporting the alleged conduct (reports, logs, witness accounts) sufficient to move it from allegation toward confirmed fact.
- Record of the employee's opportunity to respond and their response.
- Comparable-case records, to the extent available, for consistency analysis.
- Timeline evidence relative to any protected activity, for retaliation-risk screening.

## Exceptions
- Immediate protective action (e.g., temporary separation of parties pending investigation) is not itself a disciplinary measure against the accused and does not require the same finalized-measure process — but should still be proportionate, time-bounded, and non-punitive in framing until facts are established.
- A measure reaching dismissal-level severity is not an HR-process decision alone — it requires the legal-validity check in `labor-law-br`'s `discipline.md` (Art. 482 grounds, imediatidade, non bis in idem) before being finalized.

## Risk Considerations
- Recommending a severe measure based on unresolved allegation-level facts, without a documented right-to-respond step, creates both fairness and litigation exposure.
- Inconsistent treatment of comparable conduct across employees is a recurring source of discrimination and unfair-treatment claims, independent of whether any single measure was individually defensible.
- A disciplinary action that follows closely after a protected activity, without a clearly documented independent basis, is a common and high-risk retaliation fact pattern.

## Human Escalation Conditions
Escalate for human review whenever: the recommended measure is suspension or dismissal-track; the conduct is disputed or evidentially thin; a retaliation risk is plausible; the conduct also implicates harassment or discrimination (route to those references in this skill); or consistency with prior cases cannot be confirmed.

## Freshness Requirements
Standard for the HR-process principles described here (proportionality, consistency, right to respond are stable doctrinal/practice expectations); defer to `labor-law-br`'s `discipline.md` freshness requirements (critical) for any legal-validity conclusion.

## Effective-Date Considerations
The HR-process principles in this reference are not tied to a specific statutory effective date; they reflect stable good-practice and doctrinal expectations. Any legal-validity conclusion about a specific measure's basis under CLT Art. 482 must use `labor-law-br`'s `discipline.md`, which carries its own effective-date considerations.

## Known Limitations
- Does not itself resolve whether specific conduct meets a CLT Art. 482 legal threshold — that determination requires `labor-law-br`'s `discipline.md` and, for consequential cases, human legal review.
- Does not cover the mechanics of a formal internal investigation (interview protocol, evidence chain) — see `investigation.md` in this skill and the `corporate-investigation` skill for cases warranting that level of process.
- "Comparable prior cases" analysis depends entirely on the organization's actual case history, which is `CORPORATE_CONTEXT_REQUIRED` and cannot be assumed or fabricated.
