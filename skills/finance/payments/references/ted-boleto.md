# TED and Boleto

## Topics Covered
- TED
- boleto

## Purpose
Establish TED- and boleto-specific preparation and validation concerns — settlement timing, boleto tampering risk, and the fraud patterns specific to each rail — before preparing (never approving or executing) a payment through either mechanism.

## When to Load
Load whenever a request involves preparing, validating, or reviewing a TED transfer or a boleto payment — before recommending either be prepared for approval.

## Scope
Covers TED (Transferência Eletrônica Disponível) and boleto bancário as payment rails, including their specific fraud-risk profiles. Does not cover the general vendor-payment/bank-detail-change control framework (see `vendor-payments-controls.md`), Pix (see `pix.md`), or CNAB-batch mechanics (see `batch-cnab.md`), which boleto and TED processing commonly rely on for volume.

## Core Concepts
- **TED**: an electronic transfer between different financial institutions, historically same-business-day settlement within banking hours (subject to cutoff times) — while Pix has displaced much of TED's traditional use case for speed, TED remains relevant for specific scenarios (e.g., certain business/scheduled transfers, amounts or contexts where a Pix-equivalent flow is not used). Like Pix, once settled, a TED is not simply recallable by the sender — a receiving-bank-mediated return process is required, and cooperation is not guaranteed.
- **Boleto bancário**: a payment instrument (with a registered barcode/linha digitável) generated by the beneficiary (or a service on the beneficiary's behalf), payable through the paying entity's bank or authorized channel — used heavily for vendor invoices, especially recurring or registered ones.
- **Boleto tampering — the primary fraud pattern**: a manipulated boleto (a fraudulent PDF, an intercepted email, or a compromised link) can display a beneficiary name that matches the legitimate vendor while the actual linha digitável/barcode routes payment to a fraudster's account — this is one of the most common and financially significant payment-fraud patterns specifically because it exploits the visual trust of a familiar-looking document. Verification requires validating the actual barcode/linha digitável's registered beneficiary through the paying bank's own validation, not merely reading the vendor name printed on the PDF.
- **Boleto registrado (registered boleto)**: since the FEBRABAN-mandated boleto registration requirement, every boleto must be registered with the issuing bank before circulation, allowing the paying bank to validate a boleto's actual current data (beneficiary, amount, status) against the registered record at the moment of payment — this registered-data check is the actual defense against a tampered PDF, not visual inspection of the document itself.
- **Cross-rail volume via CNAB**: both TED (for batch transfers) and boleto (registration/settlement) are commonly processed in volume through CNAB remessa/retorno files rather than one-off manual entry — see `batch-cnab.md` for the file-level controls this implies.

## Decision Points
1. For a boleto payment: has the payment amount/beneficiary been validated against the bank's registered-boleto data (not merely the printed PDF), immediately before payment preparation?
2. For a TED: is the receiving account's ownership consistent with the expected payee, using the same independent-verification discipline as any other payment rail (see `vendor-payments-controls.md`)?
3. Does the boleto or TED relate to a recently-changed or newly-registered payee/bank detail requiring independent-channel confirmation before preparation proceeds?
4. Is the boleto being paid close to its due date under unusual urgency (a common social-engineering pressure tactic), warranting extra scrutiny rather than expedited processing?

## Required Facts
- The boleto's registered beneficiary/amount data, validated against the bank's own record, not the PDF alone.
- The TED receiving account's ownership data.
- Whether either payment ties to a recently-changed bank detail.

## Required Evidence
- The bank's registered-boleto validation output (beneficiary, amount, status) at the time of payment preparation.
- Independent-channel confirmation record for any new/changed bank detail (see `vendor-payments-controls.md`).
- The original invoice/contract the boleto or TED is meant to satisfy, for amount/payee cross-check.

## Exceptions
- A boleto with a registered-data mismatch against the printed document is not automatically fraud — it can reflect a legitimate reissue or correction — but it is never something to resolve by trusting the printed document over the registered data; always resolve via the beneficiary's independently-confirmed instruction, not the boleto document itself.
- A same-vendor boleto paid routinely for months does not exempt a specific instance from registered-data validation — fraud commonly targets exactly this kind of routine, lower-scrutiny payment.

## Risk Considerations
- Relying on the printed/PDF boleto's visual appearance (logo, vendor name, layout) rather than the bank's registered-data validation is the single most exploited weakness in boleto-based payment fraud.
- Treating urgency ("pay today or service is cut off") as a reason to skip standard validation is a common social-engineering pressure pattern, not a legitimate reason to bypass controls.
- Assuming TED's relative slowness (compared to Pix) provides a meaningful fraud-recovery window is unreliable — recovery still depends on the receiving institution's cooperation, not an automatic reversal right.

## Human Escalation Conditions
Escalate for human review whenever: a boleto's registered data does not match its printed/PDF appearance; a TED or boleto ties to a recently-changed bank detail without completed independent confirmation; or unusual urgency accompanies either payment type.

## Effective-Date Considerations
Boleto registration is now a settled, mandatory requirement (per the FEBRABAN-led national boleto-registration initiative) — an older assumption that a boleto could be validated purely by its printed appearance, without checking registered data, reflects a pre-registration-era practice that should not be applied to current payment preparation.
